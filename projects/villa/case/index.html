Villa Sereine

A booking site for a private villa above Monaco. Four bedrooms, a pool, the whole house rented to one group. The site lets a guest walk through the house, look at the view, and see the full price for their dates before they ever write to anyone.

RoleNaming, brand, UX, UI, motion, front-end
ScopeLogo, landing page, booking calendar
StackHTML, CSS, JS, GSAP
Year2026

The brief

A single-storey villa near Èze for up to eight guests, €1,000 a night from October to May and €1,300 in summer. The owner wanted booking that works like Booking.com: pick dates, see what is free, see the total and the deposit. Extras on request: a chef, an airport transfer, a day on a yacht.

What guests fear

Paying a lot for a house they have only seen in a few photos, and finding hidden costs at the end.

What they come for

The view. Every decision on the page puts the sea and Monaco in front of the guest as early and as often as possible.

Balance

Quiet luxury first, one strong moment of motion. Nothing on the page competes with the photos or with the button that books.

A name and a window

Sereine means calm in French and was free across brands, domains and social handles. The mark is an arched window with the sun on the sea horizon. It became the shape of the whole site: the first screen, the bedroom cards and the map pin.

Structure first

A black and white wireframe set the order: the view, the rooms, the price, the calendar, then everything that removes doubt. Location, reviews and a short list of questions come after the guest already knows what they would pay.

Opening from the horizon

On scroll the window fades, a thin line is drawn along the horizon and the sea opens up and down from it, like the sun in the logo. A quiet sign beside the window tells first-time visitors that the page moves when they scroll.

A walk through the house

Five rooms on one pinned stage. Each one slides in like the glass wall of the living room, led by a thin line of light, while the room you leave drifts back. The page colour never flashes between photos, and a button lets anyone skip straight to the dates.

One house in every photo

All images were generated from one shared description of the villa: the same limestone, travertine, bronze arched doors, oak beams and the same view. Four bedrooms differ only in what you see through the door.

A price you can trust

The calendar shows booked nights, the summer rate and how many nights are still free. The total and the deposit update as you choose, together with the price per guest: €125 a night each when eight share the house. The two cards keep one height whatever you click.

On a phone

A price and a button stay at the bottom of the screen, rooms keep their captions above it, and the calendar shows one month at a time.

Honest notes

A concept for my portfolio, not a live client. The photos, reviews and details such as the pool length are generated to show how the real ones would sit on the page.
