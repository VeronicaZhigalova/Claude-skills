# Make it look human (Veronika's rule for every project)

Veronika works with an AI assistant, but every piece of work must look as if a professional designer made it. Read this file before you start and check against it before you hand anything over. The goal is to bring the "AI look" down as far as it can go. It cannot be fully removed, but it must be as low as possible.

## Presentation
- No device mockup in the middle of the page with lists on either side. A designer lays screens out flat in a row, as scenario steps on a board, the way Figma looks.
- No slogan headings around the work: no "One place for everything…", "Every state is designed", "Seamless experience". The work's frame is a file label (name · part · version · date) in mono plus numbered steps.
- Explanations are short designer notes: a specific "why" tied to one screen. Never invent research ("owners asked…", "most people…") when there was none.
- Remove anything that announces the process ("This is a prototype", "Try the screens").

## Copy
- Ground it in one specific place: an address, the people's names, small details ("the green door", "the courtyard if the gate's open").
- Each character has their own voice: the vet is short and exact; the groomer or desk is warm and a little funny; the system is plain.
- The same sentence never appears on two screens. No generic CTAs like "Book now and we'll remind you".
- Replies and messages respond to what was actually asked.
- Run the copy through stop-slop and humanizer: no "no X, no Y, no Z" triplets, no pretty empty phrases.

## Data
- Uneven numbers: 61%, 4.2%, €2,180. Allow a bad day with a reason (−4%, two cancellations).
- Real exceptions: a client without the app (a "Call" button instead of "Send"), someone running late, a training block in the schedule, a staff member leaving early.
- Never give every metric a neat positive delta.

## Ideas
- At least one UX idea of your own that competitors don't have and that comes from this specific client's life. Standard patterns are fine underneath, but they must not be the whole story.

## Motion
- Give it character. Brand or personal things and service things move differently, and the difference is written into the design system (curves and durations).
- Do not use the default set alone (fade-up, the same 0.45 s everywhere).

## Icons and logo
- Draw your own in the project's style (stroke ends, weight, a shape taken from the client's world). No stock line icon set.
- The logo is an idea from the client's world (the dog tag for Fenn), not a circle with a sprig.

## Photos
- Use generated photos only when there is no real shoot, and write the prompts as an ordinary phone photo by a real person: "ordinary iPhone photo taken by the owner", a slightly tilted horizon, natural light, a little noise, everyday clutter in the frame (a charger, a remote, a mug, fur on the table), an unposed pet or person.
- Banned: studio backdrops, perfect symmetry, "editorial", "cinematic", glow, a lone object on a plain background (unless it is a product shot).
- In the case study, say honestly that the photos are generated.

## Style
- Do not use the default set "cream background + soft serif + sage + identical rounded cards".
- One strong move instead of an even row of identical blocks.
- Check the feel against project-log.md, not just the hex codes.

## Before handing over
Score the work out loud from 0–100% "looks like AI" and name the specific tells. If anything above 20% can be fixed, fix it before handing over.
