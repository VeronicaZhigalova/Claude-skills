# Prototype kit (Figma Cc4CPMe2dcfGsX6PGYLBbo): Vistora / @bee_ui.ux

Veronika's main visual library. Read it before choosing a direction. Every project is shown in two versions: a **B/W wireframe** (the layout skeleton) and **"with images"** (the finished colour design). Take the **principle** of the layout and the system, never copy the picture.

## How these sites are built (the shared language of the kit)
1. **The first screen is a frame object**: a card with a thick light border and a large radius, standing "in a scene". It is not a phone or browser mockup.
2. **Notches in the frame**: concave cut-outs on the edge that hold the menu, logo, CTA or a promo card (inverted corners).
3. **The scene continues the photo**: the background behind the frame is the brand's world (hills, stone, sky, silk, desert, curtain). The site stands inside it.
4. **The product breaks the frame**: the object goes past the edge or stands between the letters (For[chair]mo, the lamp over ONDERA, the cyclist over OWN THE COURT).
5. **Ghost type**: a huge pale word behind the product, and a huge wordmark in the footer (ampora, HAVEN, SMASH BURGER, LOMORA, biomos, METORA).
6. **Bento of uneven weight**: one big cell plus 2–4 small ones, with numbers as content (98.7%, 70k+, 80+).
7. **Diagonal cut** on a big cell: movement without animation.
8. **Vertical rail menu** of icons on the left, with a round CTA at the bottom.
9. **Circles at the seams**: an arrow button on the border between blocks, round previews, indicators.
10. **Floating proof**: a mini card (rating, 100k+ with avatars, next item, price) on top of the photo.
11. **One strong palette**: monochrome plus one saturated colour taken from the photo, with the scene background in the same colour.
12. **Wireframe → design** as the case-study format: the grey grid first, then the finished screen.

Rhythm of a full site (desktop, plus mobile next to it in many projects): hero frame → manifesto/fact line → product grid or bento → a big photo with a block of text → reviews or a quote with a portrait → FAQ/subscription → a dark footer card with a giant wordmark.

## How to use it in projects
- At the start, pick 1–2 systems from the kit that are **closest in mood** to the niche, plus 1 that is far away (for a fresh move). Write it in the brief: "From the kit: Aurora (italic intro word + portrait), Zumi (numbered steps, ingredient ribbon)."
- Always take **at least 3 of the 12 techniques**: they are what gives the "zing". A frame with notches + a scene + a product over the type is the base of the language.
- The palette is built the kit's way: 6 colours from the real photos, one saturated accent, the scene background in the same colour.
- A giant wordmark in the footer and a floating proof card are used almost everywhere. They are a cheap and strong way to add character.
- Before handing over, compare against the kit: if the site looks like an "ordinary landing page" next to it, the frame/scene/product-breaks-the-frame layer is missing.

## My own concepts (page «Новые концепты (Claude)» in the kit, files in projects/concepts/)
New principle in the kit's language: **the frame is an object from the client's world**.
Approved: Kiln & Co (clay slab, fingerprint), Sève (wine label on a bottle), Petal House (stem through the notches), Hale (the frame is the reformer). Löyly House v2: a matte card on a sauna shelf (v1 was 50/50).
**Rejected as "AI-looking"** (Veronika, 22.10): Crumb (bread between the letters), Swell (surfer on the wave), Margin (open book). Why: glossy 3D render, the object is too literal and toy-like, everything is centred and too perfect, a pretty "cloud of steam".
Rule: the concept should read like a real photo of a real object (matte, wood grain, ordinary items around it), and the UI is flat and calm. If it looks like a render or a toy, redo it.
